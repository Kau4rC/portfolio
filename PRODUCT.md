## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js 16 (App Router) · React 19 · TypeScript · Tailwind CSS v4 — inherited from the existing scaffold; deploy target Vercel.

## Users

Recruiters, hiring managers, and technical interviewers evaluating Julz Kaupper Cortes for full-stack web roles. They arrive from a resume, LinkedIn, or GitHub link and skim on a laptop between other candidates. Secondary audience: developers and collaborators checking technical depth.

## Product Purpose

A single-page personal portfolio that proves Julz can design and ship full-stack web work. Success means a recruiter can answer "who is this, what have they built, and can they do the job" in one scroll and reach contact in one click.

## Positioning

Proof before claims: three real shipped projects (Simply POS, BITS, MADJIC) lead, and the stack spans web, backend/data, and networking infrastructure. The differentiator is full-stack web delivery backed by CCNA / CyberOps networking credentials and automation work.

## Operating Context

Visited on desktop and mobile, often in bright office or library light. Content originates from `Resume.pdf` and the project work itself. Links out to email, GitHub, and LinkedIn. Hosted on Vercel.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Single page with anchor navigation.
- No backend and no contact form; contact is outbound links only.
- No phone number displayed.
- Projects section must scale from 3 to N without layout rework.
- Content must be real and defensible in an interview — no placeholder or invented projects.
- All motion gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Dark-only presentation.

## Product Principles

1. Proof before claims: projects first, skills second.
2. Earn the interview: every claim traceable to real work.
3. One scroll, zero friction: navigation and contact always reachable.
4. Read in a bright room: contrast and legibility outrank atmosphere.
5. Ship small: static-first, no backend, no dependency without a job.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA target: 4.5:1 body contrast, visible keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, semantic landmarks and headings, and no color-only meaning.
